🔍 Understanding Isrā'īliyyāt

Isrā'īliyyāt refers to stories, legends, and traditions from Jewish and Christian sources (Ahl al-Kitāb, "People of the Book") that entered Islamic literature, Qur'anic commentary (tafsīr), and history books over time. The word comes from Isrā'īl (Israel/Jacob), though scholars later expanded it to include tales from other ancient cultures too.

These narratives entered Islam mainly through early converts from Jewish and Christian communities, who brought background stories about prophets and events only briefly mentioned in the Qur'an. Muslims used them to fill in extra details, and they appear widely in tafsīr and books of Qisas al-Anbiya (Stories of the Prophets).

How Should a Muslim Treat Isrā'īliyyāt?

Scholars divide these narrations into three categories:

  • Accepted (Al-Maqbūl): Stories that agree with confirmed Islamic teachings — permissible to narrate.
  • Rejected (Al-Mardūd): Stories that contradict the Qur'an, authentic Hadith, or core Islamic beliefs — forbidden to accept or spread, except to point out that they are false.
  • Neutral (Al-Waqif): Stories that neither confirm nor deny Islamic teachings (minor details, names, dates) — scholars advise withholding judgement (tawaqquf) rather than asserting them as fact.

Throughout this lesson, every section clearly separates Qur'an / Authentic Hadith from Isrā'īliyyāt (Neutral Tradition) so that you learn to distinguish revealed truth from added folklore.

🌍 Allah's Plan for a Successor on Earth

إِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً

"When your Lord said to the angels, 'I am placing a successor on earth…'"

Qur'an 2:30

The angels asked about the possibility of corruption and bloodshed. This was not rebellion — classical tafsir explains it as a question seeking understanding of Allah's wisdom. Allah replied, "Indeed, I know that which you do not know."

Qur'an The declaration to the angels and Allah's reply are explicitly stated in the Qur'an (2:30; 6:165; 27:62).

🧱 Adam's Creation

إِنِّي خَالِقٌ بَشَرًا مِن طِينٍ فَإِذَا سَوَّيْتُهُ وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِي فَقَعُوا لَهُ سَاجِدِينَ

"I am creating a human being from clay. So when I have fashioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, then fall down before him in prostration."

Qur'an 38:71–72

Angels gathered soil of different colours and textures, and the clay was left until it became like sounding pottery. Allah then breathed the soul (Rūh) into Adam.

Qur'an / Hadith The declaration to the angels, the varied soil, the hollow clay state, and the breathing of the soul are stated in the Qur'an and authentic Hadith.
Isrā'īliyyāt Exact measurements of Adam's stature beyond the "60 cubits" hadith, and long dramatic dialogues between the angels and the un-animated clay body, are neutral Isrā'īliyyāt additions found in some tafsir works.

Qur'an 3:59 uses Adam's creation to explain that the extraordinary birth of Prophet Isa (AS) does not make him divine:

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُ مِن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ

"Indeed, the example of Jesus before Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, 'Be,' and he was."

Qur'an 3:59

🚫 Iblis's Refusal to Prostrate

وَإِذْ قُلْنَا لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ اسْجُدُوا لِآدَمَ فَسَجَدُوا إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ أَبَىٰ وَاسْتَكْبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ الْكَافِرِينَ

"When We said to the angels, 'Prostrate before Adam,' they prostrated, except for Iblis. He refused, was arrogant, and became one of the disbelievers."

Qur'an 2:34
  • The prostration was commanded by Allah as a gesture of honour and obedience — it was not worship of Adam (AS).
  • All the angels obeyed immediately; only Iblis refused.
Qur'an The command, the universal obedience of the angels, and Iblis's singular refusal are entirely Qur'anic (2:34; 7:11; 15:30; 18:50; 38:73).
Isrā'īliyyāt Elaborate backstories describing Iblis as a great general who fought rogue jinn on earth prior to Adam's creation mostly stem from Isrā'īliyyāt lore, not the Qur'an.

🔥 The Reason Behind the Refusal

قَالَ أَنَا خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ خَلَقْتَنِي مِن نَّارٍ وَخَلَقْتَهُ مِن طِينٍ

"He said, 'I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay.'"

Qur'an 7:12; see also 38:76

Iblis's refusal was rooted in arrogance and false reasoning — he judged worth by material origin rather than obedience to Allah. He did not repent, and instead vowed hostility toward Adam's descendants.

Qur'an Iblis's argument, his banishment, and his vow to mislead humanity are entirely Qur'anic (7:12–18; 15:31–43; 17:61–65).

👁️ Jinn, Iblis & Shaytan: What's the Difference?

These three terms are often confused, but each refers to something different.

TermWhat It Means
JinnA species created by Allah from smokeless fire, thousands of years before Adam. Jinn have free will, and can be righteous believers or disbelievers — like humans (Qur'an 15:27; 55:15).
IblisThe proper name of one specific jinn — the first to disobey Allah by refusing to prostrate before Adam. He is not a species or a title, but an individual (Qur'an 18:50).
ShaytanA general title (not a species or a name) for any jinn or human who rebels against Allah and misleads others. Iblis is the chief Shaytan, but any evil-inclined jinn or human can be called a shaytan.
Qur'an The distinction between jinn as a species, Iblis as an individual jinn, and shaytan as a title for rebellious beings is supported by Qur'an 18:50, 15:27, and 55:15.

🧠 Allah Teaches Adam the Names

وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى الْمَلَائِكَةِ فَقَالَ أَنبِئُونِي بِأَسْمَاءِ هَٰؤُلَاءِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَادِقِينَ

"He taught Adam the names of all things, then He presented them to the angels and said, 'Tell Me the names of these, if you are truthful.'"

Qur'an 2:31

What Were "The Names"?

The Qur'an states Allah taught Adam "the names of all things" — a knowledge the angels did not possess. Commentators differ on the precise scope, so the secure understanding is that Allah gave Adam special knowledge unavailable to the angels.

Why Did Allah Teach Him This?

  • It demonstrated Adam's special capacity to learn and showed Allah's wisdom in choosing him for a responsible role on earth.
  • The angels admitted their limits ("We have no knowledge except what You have taught us"), showing humility.
  • Knowledge is a gift from Allah, to be used with humility and moral responsibility — not for boasting, as Iblis's arrogance shows what knowledge without obedience can lead to.
Qur'an The teaching of the names and the angels' admission of limited knowledge are explicitly stated (2:31–33).

💞 The Creation of Adam's Wife

Adam lived without a companion, so Allah created his wife so that he could find comfort and tranquility.

Qur'an / Hadith The creation of a spouse for Adam is Qur'anic (Qur'an 4:1; 7:189), and an authentic Hadith corroborates that woman was created from a rib.
Isrā'īliyyāt The Qur'an never names her; it only refers to her as his zawj (spouse/pair). The name "Hawwa" (Eve) comes from Hadith and Hebrew-linked tradition, not the Qur'an itself.

🌳 Life in the Garden

وَقُلْنَا يَا آدَمُ اسْكُنْ أَنتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ وَكُلَا مِنْهَا رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا وَلَا تَقْرَبَا هَٰذِهِ الشَّجَرَةَ فَتَكُونَا مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

"We said, 'O Adam, dwell—you and your wife—in the Garden and eat freely from wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers.'"

Qur'an 2:35; see also 7:19
Qur'an The command to dwell in the Garden, enjoy its blessings, and avoid one tree is strictly Qur'anic.
Isrā'īliyyāt The Qur'an never names the fruit. The idea it was an apple (or a fig, wheat, or grapevine) comes entirely from Isrā'īliyyāt — do not state a specific fruit as fact.

🐍 Iblis's Deception

فَوَسْوَسَ لَهُمَا الشَّيْطَانُ لِيُبْدِيَ لَهُمَا مَا وُورِيَ عَنْهُمَا مِن سَوْآتِهِمَا

"Then Satan whispered to them in order to expose to them what had been hidden from them of their nakedness."

Qur'an 7:20
هَلْ أَدُلُّكَ عَلَىٰ شَجَرَةِ الْخُلْدِ وَمُلْكٍ لَّا يَبْلَىٰ

"Shall I direct you to the tree of immortality and a kingdom that will not decay?"

Qur'an 20:120

Iblis approached with false sympathy, even swearing he was a sincere adviser, convincing them the tree was forbidden only to stop them becoming immortal or angels.

Qur'an Satan's whispering and false promises are explicitly Qur'anic (7:20; 20:120).
Isrā'īliyyāt The story of Iblis hiding inside a serpent's fangs to sneak into the Garden is imported from Judeo-Christian tradition and is not found in the Qur'an or authentic Hadith.

🍃 What Happened After

فَبَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْآتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِن وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ

"Then their nakedness became apparent to them, and they began to fasten together leaves from the Garden over themselves."

Qur'an 7:22; see also 20:121

Adam and his wife forgot the warning, ate from the tree, and their nakedness was exposed. Overcome with shame, they covered themselves with leaves.

Qur'an The Qur'an places the mistake on both Adam and his wife equally — both forgot, both ate, both felt shame (7:22; 20:121).
Isrā'īliyyāt Older tafsir entries claiming Eve tempted Adam or ate first are rejected — the Qur'an clearly states the mistake happened simultaneously to both.

🤲 Adam's Repentance

قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ

"They said, 'Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers.'"

Qur'an 7:23
فَتَلَقَّىٰ آدَمُ مِن رَّبِّهِ كَلِمَاتٍ فَتَابَ عَلَيْهِ ۚ إِنَّهُ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ

"Then Adam received words from his Lord, and Allah accepted his repentance. Indeed, He is the Accepting of repentance, the Merciful."

Qur'an 2:37

Unlike Iblis, who doubled down on arrogance, Adam and his wife felt instant remorse and turned to Allah. Islam teaches personal accountability, not inherited sin — Adam's mistake was fully forgiven, and there is no concept of "original sin" in Islam.

Qur'an The prayer of repentance and Allah's complete forgiveness are explicit Qur'anic pillars (2:37; 7:23; 20:122).

🌏 The Descent to Earth

قَالَ اهْبِطُوا بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ ۖ وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ

"He said, 'Go down, some of you as enemies to others. On earth you will have a place of settlement and provision for a time.'"

Qur'an 7:24; see also 2:36

Allah commanded Adam, his wife, and Iblis to descend to earth. Despite Adam's forgiveness, this transition to earth had always been the plan announced before his creation — not a punishment.

Qur'an The decree to live on earth and Allah's promise of guidance (2:38–39; 20:123–124) are explicit Qur'anic pillars.
Isrā'īliyyāt Claims that Adam landed in Sri Lanka and his wife in Jeddah, wandering separately for 200 years before reuniting at Mount Arafat, are pure Isrā'īliyyāt with no basis in the Qur'an or authentic Hadith.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Life on Earth as a Family

Adam was the first human and the first Prophet, teaching his children the worship of One God (Tawhid).

Qur'an Adam's status as the first prophet and the origin of humanity's shared descent is supported by Qur'an 3:33; 4:1; 49:13.
Isrā'īliyyāt Detailed accounts of Adam's daily domestic life, exact family size, and specific household events beyond the Qur'an's general statements are largely drawn from later tradition.

⚖️ The Story of Qabil and Habil

Adam's two sons each offered a sacrifice to Allah. One sacrifice was accepted because of piety; the other was rejected. Out of jealousy, the rejected brother murdered the other — the first murder in human history.

Qur'an The story of the two sons, the acceptance/rejection of their sacrifices, and the murder are found in the Qur'an (Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:27–31). However, the Qur'an refers to them only as "the two sons of Adam," without naming them.
Isrā'īliyyāt The names "Qabil" and "Habil" come from Isrā'īliyyāt, as does the famous tradition that Eve bore twins (a boy and girl) each pregnancy, and that Qabil killed Habil over a dispute about marrying his twin sister. This is classified as neutral tradition.
