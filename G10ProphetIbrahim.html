The Story of Prophet Ibrahim (AS)

A chronological study of tawḥīd, courage, trust in Allah, sacrifice and obedience. AS means “peace be upon him”.

Introduction: Who Ibrahim (AS) is

Prophet Ibrahim (AS) is one of Allah’s greatest messengers. He called people to worship Allah alone and reject every false object of worship. The Qur’an describes him as a khalīl, an intimate friend of Allah, and presents him as an upright leader and a model for believers.

وَٱتَّخَذَ ٱللَّهُ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ خَلِيلًا

“And Allah took Abraham as an intimate friend.”

Qur’an Al-Nisā’ [4:125]

إِنَّ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ كَانَ أُمَّةًۭ قَانِتًۭا لِّلَّهِ حَنِيفًۭا وَلَمْ يَكُ مِنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ۝ شَاكِرًۭا لِّأَنْعُمِهِۦ ۚ ٱجْتَبَىٰهُ وَهَدَىٰهُ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ

“Indeed, Abraham was a model of devotion to Allah, true in faith, and he was not of those who associated partners with Allah. He was grateful for His favours. Allah chose him and guided him to a straight path.”

Qur’an Al-Naḥl [16:120–121]
Tawḥīd
Worshipping Allah alone, without partners.
Khalīl Allah
Allah’s intimate friend; a title of honour.
Hanīf
One who turns away from shirk towards pure monotheism.
Al-Bayt
The Sacred House: the Ka‘bah in Makkah.

Early life and rejection of idols

Ibrahim (AS) lived among people who worshipped idols. The Qur’an records his respectful but firm challenge to his father, Āzar, and his rejection of inherited false beliefs. The lesson is not disrespect for parents; it is that obedience to Allah comes before obedience to any human being.

وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَٰهِيمُ لِأَبِيهِ ءَازَرَ أَتَتَّخِذُ أَصْنَامًا ءَالِهَةً ۖ إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰكَ وَقَوْمَكَ فِى ضَلَـٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ ۝ وَكَذَٰلِكَ نُرِىٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ مَلَكُوتَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَلِيَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلْمُوقِنِينَ

“And remember when Abraham said to his father, Āzar, ‘Do you take idols as gods? Indeed, I see you and your people are clearly astray.’ And thus We showed Abraham the kingdom of the heavens and the earth so that he would be among those who had sure faith.”

Qur’an Al-Anʿām [6:74–75]

Searching for the Creator

The Qur’an describes Ibrahim’s reasoning through the setting of a star, the moon and the sun. Each disappears, so none can be the eternal Lord. He then declares his complete devotion to the One who created the heavens and the earth.

فَلَمَّا جَنَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلَّيْلُ رَءَا كَوْكَبًا ۖ قَالَ هَٰذَا رَبِّى ۖ فَلَمَّآ أَفَلَ قَالَ لَآ أُحِبُّ ٱلْـَٔافِلِينَ ۝ فَلَمَّا رَءَا ٱلْقَمَرَ بَازِغًۭا قَالَ هَٰذَا رَبِّى ۖ فَلَمَّآ أَفَلَ قَالَ لَئِن لَّمْ يَهْدِنِى رَبِّى لَأَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلضَّآلِّينَ ۝ فَلَمَّا رَءَا ٱلشَّمْسَ بَازِغَةًۭ قَالَ هَٰذَا رَبِّى هَٰذَآ أَكْبَرُ ۖ فَلَمَّآ أَفَلَتْ قَالَ يَـٰقَوْمِ إِنِّى بَرِىٓءٌۭ مِّمَّا تُشْرِكُونَ ۝ إِنِّى وَجَّهْتُ وَجْهِىَ لِلَّذِى فَطَرَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ حَنِيفًۭا ۖ وَمَآ أَنَا۠ مِنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ

“When the night covered him, he saw a star and said, ‘This is my Lord.’ But when it set, he said, ‘I do not love things that set.’ Then when he saw the moon rising, he said, ‘This is my Lord.’ But when it set, he said, ‘If my Lord does not guide me, I will certainly be among the misguided people.’ Then when he saw the sun shining, he said, ‘This must be my Lord—it is أكبر (greater)!’ But when it set, he said, ‘O my people! I am free of whatever you associate with Allah. I have turned my face towards the One who created the heavens and the earth, being ever inclined to faith, and I am not one of those who associate partners with Allah.’”

Qur’an Al-Anʿām [6:76–79]

Calling to tawḥīd and debating his people

Ibrahim (AS) called his people to use reason and to worship the One who created them. When they relied on ancestral custom, he showed that tradition cannot make a powerless object worthy of worship.

إِذْ قَالَ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِۦ مَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلتَّمَاثِيلُ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنتُمْ لَهَا عَـٰكِفُونَ ۝ قَالُوا۟ وَجَدْنَآ ءَابَآءَنَا لَهَا عَـٰبِدِينَ ۝ قَالَ لَقَدْ كُنتُمْ أَنتُمْ وَءَابَآؤُكُمْ فِى ضَلَـٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ

“When he said to his father and his people, ‘What are these statues to which you are devoted?’ They replied, ‘We found our forefathers worshipping them.’ He said, ‘Indeed, you and your forefathers have been clearly astray.’”

Qur’an Al-Anbiyā’ [21:52–54]

Breaking the idols

When the people left for their festival, Ibrahim (AS) broke the idols, leaving the largest one. When they questioned him, he directed them to recognise the obvious: idols cannot speak, benefit or defend themselves.

قَالُوا۟ ءَأَنتَ فَعَلْتَ هَٰذَا بِـَٔالِهَتِنَا يَـٰٓإِبْرَٰهِيمُ ۝ قَالَ بَلْ فَعَلَهُۥ كَبِيرُهُمْ هَٰذَا فَسْـَٔلُوهُمْ إِن كَانُوا۟ يَنطِقُونَ ۝ فَرَجَعُوٓا۟ إِلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ فَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّكُمْ أَنتُمُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمُونَ ۝ ثُمَّ نُكِسُوا۟ عَلَىٰ رُءُوسِهِمْ لَقَدْ عَلِمْتَ مَا هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ يَنطِقُونَ

“They asked, ‘Have you done this to our gods, O Abraham?’ He replied, ‘No, this one—the largest of them—did it. Ask them, if they can speak!’ So they turned to one another, saying, ‘Indeed, you yourselves are the wrongdoers.’ Then they hung their heads in shame and said, ‘You certainly know that they cannot speak.’”

Qur’an Al-Anbiyā’ [21:62–65]

Debate with the king

The Qur’an also records Ibrahim’s debate with a ruler who argued with him about Allah. Ibrahim (AS) moved from the ruler’s claim about giving life and death to a clear challenge about the rising of the sun.

أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى ٱلَّذِى حَآجَّ إِبْرَٰهِـۧمَ فِى رَبِّهِۦٓ أَنْ ءَاتَىٰهُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُلْكَ إِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَٰهِـۧمُ رَبِّىَ ٱلَّذِى يُحْىِۦ وَيُمِيتُ ۖ قَالَ أَنَا۠ أُحْىِۦ وَأُمِيتُ ۖ قَالَ إِبْرَٰهِـۧمُ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَأْتِى بِٱلشَّمْسِ مِنَ ٱلْمَشْرِقِ فَأْتِ بِهَا مِنَ ٱلْمَغْرِبِ فَبُهِتَ ٱلَّذِى كَفَرَ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ لَا يَهْدِى ٱلْقَوْمَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ

“Have you not seen the one who argued with Abraham about his Lord because Allah had given him kingship? Abraham said, ‘My Lord is the One who gives life and causes death.’ He argued, ‘I give life and cause death.’ Abraham said, ‘Allah brings the sun from the east, so bring it from the west.’ Thus the disbeliever was dumbfounded. And Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people.”

Qur’an Al-Baqarah [2:258]

Migration from his homeland

After persistent rejection, Ibrahim (AS) migrated for the sake of Allah. Migration here means leaving a place of danger and disbelief in order to preserve faith and continue serving Allah. His life shows that sacrifice for truth may involve leaving familiar people and places.

وَقَالَ إِنِّى ذَاهِبٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّى سَيَهْدِينِ ۝ رَبِّ هَبْ لِى مِنَ ٱلصَّـٰلِحِينَ

“And he said, ‘I am going to my Lord. He will guide me. My Lord, grant me a righteous child.’”

Qur’an Al-Ṣāffāt [37:99–100]

Allah granted him a family and a continuing mission. The Qur’an later describes Ibrahim (AS) as leaving his family in a barren valley near the Sacred House and praying for its security and for hearts to incline towards them.

رَّبَّنَآ إِنِّىٓ أَسْكَنتُ مِن ذُرِّيَّتِى بِوَادٍ غَيْرِ ذِى زَرْعٍ عِندَ بَيْتِكَ ٱلْمُحَرَّمِ رَبَّنَا لِيُقِيمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ فَٱجْعَلْ أَفْـِٔدَةًۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ تَهْوِىٓ إِلَيْهِمْ وَٱرْزُقْهُم مِّنَ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَشْكُرُونَ

“Our Lord! I have settled some of my offspring in a barren valley near Your Sacred House, our Lord, so that they may establish prayer. So make the hearts of some people incline towards them and provide them with fruits, so perhaps they will be grateful.”

Qur’an Ibrāhīm [14:37]

Trials and tests

🔥 The fire

The people responded to Ibrahim’s rejection of idols by deciding to burn him. Allah protected His prophet through a clear miracle: the fire was commanded to become cool and safe.

قَالُوا۟ حَرِّقُوهُ وَٱنصُرُوٓا۟ ءَالِهَتَكُمْ إِن كُنتُمْ فَـٰعِلِينَ ۝ قُلْنَا يَـٰنَارُ كُونِى بَرْدًۭا وَسَلَـٰمًا عَلَىٰٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ ۝ وَأَرَادُوا۟ بِهِۦ كَيْدًۭا فَجَعَلْنَـٰهُمُ ٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ

“They said, ‘Burn him and support your gods, if you are going to do anything.’ We said, ‘O fire! Be cool and safe for Abraham.’ They planned against him, but We made them the biggest losers.”

Qur’an Al-Anbiyā’ [21:68–70]

“The Prophet ﷺ said that the salamander used to blow on the fire of Abraham.”

Hadith – Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Anbiyāʾ, ḥadīth no. 3359

🤲 Hajar, Ismāʿīl and Makkah

Ibrahim (AS) left Hajar and the infant Ismāʿīl in the valley by Allah’s command. When Hajar asked whether Allah had commanded it, Ibrahim confirmed that He had. Hajar responded with trust that Allah would not abandon them. When their water finished, she searched for help between Ṣafā and Marwah. Allah provided Zamzam, and this event is remembered in the saʿy of Hajj and ʿUmrah.

“Ibrahim brought Hajar and her son Ismāʿīl to a place where there was no water. Hajar asked, ‘Has Allah commanded you to do this?’ He replied that He had. She said, ‘Then He will not neglect us.’ The angel struck the ground and water sprang forth; this was Zamzam.”

Hadith – Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Anbiyāʾ, ḥadīth no. 3364 (long report narrated by Ibn ʿAbbās)

Teaching point: Tawakkul is not passive. Hajar trusted Allah while actively searching for water, and Allah opened a way through her effort.

The vision of sacrificing his son

When the son had grown enough to work with his father, Ibrahim (AS) saw in a true prophetic dream that he should sacrifice him. He told his son openly, and the son willingly submitted to Allah’s command. The Qur’an identifies the son in this passage by the majority Sunni scholarly understanding as Ismāʿīl (AS).

فَلَمَّا بَلَغَ مَعَهُ ٱلسَّعْىَ قَالَ يَـٰبُنَىَّ إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰ فِى ٱلْمَنَامِ أَنِّىٓ أَذْبَحُكَ فَٱنظُرْ مَاذَا تَرَىٰ ۚ قَالَ يَـٰٓأَبَتِ ٱفْعَلْ مَا تُؤْمَرُ ۖ سَتَجِدُنِىٓ إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ مِنَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ ۝ فَلَمَّآ أَسْلَمَا وَتَلَّهُۥ لِلْجَبِينِ ۝ وَنَـٰدَيْنَـٰهُ أَن يَـٰٓإِبْرَٰهِيمُ ۝ قَدْ صَدَّقْتَ ٱلرُّءْيَآ ۚ إِنَّا كَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِى ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ ۝ إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ ٱلْبَلَـٰٓؤُا۟ ٱلْمُبِينُ ۝ وَفَدَيْنَـٰهُ بِذِبْحٍ عَظِيمٍۢ

“When the boy reached the age to work with him, Abraham said, ‘O my dear son! I have seen in a dream that I must sacrifice you. So tell me what you think.’ He replied, ‘O my dear father! Do as you are commanded. Allah willing, you will find me steadfast.’ Then when they submitted and he laid him on his forehead, We called out to him, ‘O Abraham! You have fulfilled the dream.’ Indeed, this is how We reward the good-doers. Surely this was a clear test. And We ransomed him with a great sacrifice.”

Qur’an Al-Ṣāffāt [37:102–107]

This event is remembered at ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā. The purpose is not that Allah needs blood or meat; rather, the central lesson is sincere obedience and submission.

لَن يَنَالَ ٱللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَآؤُهَا وَلَـٰكِن يَنَالُهُ ٱلتَّقْوَىٰ مِنكُمْ

“Neither their meat nor blood reaches Allah. Rather, it is your piety that reaches Him.”

Qur’an Al-Ḥajj [22:37]

Building the Kaʿbah with Ismāʿīl 🕋

Ibrahim (AS) and Ismāʿīl (AS) raised the foundations of the Sacred House. Even while doing this great work, they remained humble and asked Allah to accept it. The Kaʿbah became a centre of worship and pilgrimage for those who submit to Allah.

وَإِذْ يَرْفَعُ إِبْرَٰهِـۧمُ ٱلْقَوَاعِدَ مِنَ ٱلْبَيْتِ وَإِسْمَـٰعِيلُ رَبَّنَا تَقَبَّلْ مِنَّآ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ ۝ رَبَّنَا وَٱجْعَلْنَا مُسْلِمَيْنِ لَكَ وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِنَآ أُمَّةًۭ مُّسْلِمَةًۭ لَّكَ وَأَرِنَا مَنَاسِكَنَا وَتُبْ عَلَيْنَآ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلتَّوَّابُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ ۝ رَبَّنَا وَٱبْعَثْ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَـٰتِكَ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ

“And remember when Abraham raised the foundations of the House with Ishmael, praying, ‘Our Lord! Accept this from us! You are indeed the All-Hearing, All-Knowing. Our Lord! Make us fully submit to You and raise from our offspring a nation that will submit to You. Show us our rites and turn to us in forgiveness. You are truly the Most Relenting, Most Merciful. Our Lord! Raise among them a messenger from their own, who will recite to them Your revelations, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. You are truly the Almighty, All-Wise.’”

Qur’an Al-Baqarah [2:127–129]

“I saw Moses, Jesus and Abraham. Abraham was of medium height, with a reddish complexion and a broad chest.”

Hadith – Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Anbiyāʾ, ḥadīth no. 3354

Legacy: a model of faith

Ibrahim’s (AS) legacy continues through the prophets descended from him, the Kaʿbah, Hajj, Zamzam, the sacrifice of ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā and the call to pure monotheism. Muslims are instructed to follow his upright way: worship Allah alone, reject shirk, remain thankful, and submit sincerely.

قُلْ صَدَقَ ٱللَّهُ ۗ فَٱتَّبِعُوا۟ مِلَّةَ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ حَنِيفًۭا ۖ وَمَا كَانَ مِنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ

“Say, ‘Allah has spoken the truth. So follow the way of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists.’”

Qur’an Āl ʿImrān [3:95]

إِنَّ أَوْلَى ٱلنَّاسِ بِإِبْرَٰهِيمَ لَلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوهُ وَهَـٰذَا ٱلنَّبِىُّ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ وَلِىُّ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ

“Indeed, the people who are closest to Abraham are those who follow him, this Prophet, and the believers. And Allah is the Guardian of the believers.”

Qur’an Āl ʿImrān [3:68]

“Every prophet has an ally among the prophets, and my ally is my father Abraham, the intimate friend of my Lord.”

Hadith – Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī, Tafsīr al-Qur’an, ḥadīth no. 2995; graded authentic by scholars

Chronological overview

1. Early life: Ibrahim (AS) grows among idol-worshippers and seeks the truth.
2. Tawḥīd: He rejects celestial objects and idols as gods and calls his people to Allah alone.
3. Confrontation: He debates his people, breaks the idols and debates the ruler.
4. The fire: His people throw him into the fire, but Allah makes it cool and safe.
5. Migration: He leaves his homeland and continues his mission.
6. Makkah: Hajar and Ismāʿīl are left by Allah’s command; Zamzam appears.
7. Sacrifice: Ibrahim and Ismāʿīl submit to the test, and Allah provides a ransom.
8. The Kaʿbah: Ibrahim and Ismāʿīl raise the foundations and pray for acceptance.

Key lessons

  • Use clear evidence and sound reasoning when searching for truth.
  • Worship belongs to Allah alone; no created thing deserves worship.
  • Stand for truth with wisdom and courage, even when a majority disagrees.
  • Trust Allah while taking responsible action, as Hajar did.
  • True faith includes submission, patience, gratitude and humility.
  • Good actions should be accompanied by a sincere prayer that Allah accepts them.
