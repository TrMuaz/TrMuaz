Cambridge O Level • 2068 • 2025–2027

Islamic Studies Syllabus Guide

A detailed, student-friendly overview of the six syllabus sections, assessment structure, prescribed surahs and hadith, key teachings, and revision checklist.

Assessment at a glance

  • Two written papers, each 1 hour 30 minutes.
  • Each paper: 60 marks and 50% of the qualification.
  • Paper 1: History and Scriptures.
  • Paper 2: Development, Sources, Beliefs and Observances.
  • AO1 knowledge and understanding: 67%.
  • AO2 evaluation: 33%.

How to use this page

Open each topic to see the detailed teaching points. Use the search box to filter the page, then tick the revision checklist as topics are completed.

Exam reminder: In Paper 1 Section C, candidates choose either the prescribed surah question or the prescribed hadith question.

Paper 1 — History and Scriptures

1. Religions, traditions and practices before the prophethood

1.1 Arabia in the pre-Islamic period

  • Tribal and political organisation: Bedouin, Quraysh, leadership and social relationships.
  • Social and economic conditions: Makkah trade, pilgrim trade, slavery and infanticide.
  • Religious groups: polytheists, monotheists, their beliefs and practices.
  • Hanifs: people following the religion of Prophet Ibrahim (AS).
  • Kaʿba: significance as a centre of pilgrimage and worship.

1.2 Prophets before Muhammad (pbuh)

  • Adam (AS): creation, Garden of Eden, coming to Earth, family experiences and lessons.
  • Musa (AS): upbringing in Pharaoh’s household, prophethood, confrontation with Pharaoh, miracles and escape from Egypt.
  • Yusuf (AS): jealousy of brothers, separation, Egypt, Zulaikhah, imprisonment, freedom and reunion.
  • ʿIsa (AS): virgin birth, speech miracle, Temple, ministry and miracles.
  • Ibrahim (AS): rejection of idols, search for truth, escape from fire, dialogue with the king and test involving Ishmaʿil.
2. Prophet Muhammad (pbuh)

2.1 Makkan period

  • Early life from birth to the call to prophethood.
  • First revelation in Cave Hiraʾ and subsequent preaching.
  • Opposition, persecution and reasons for rejection of the message.
  • Responses to opponents, exile to the valley, Taʾif and hijrah to Madinah.

2.2 Madinan period

  • Leadership of Madinah, Constitution of Madinah and relations with other religious groups.
  • Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, achievements and community development.
  • Causes, events and consequences of Badr, Uhud and the Trench.
  • Conquest of Makkah, Farewell Hajj and Farewell Address.

2.3 Whole life

  • Patience and forgiveness when dealing with opponents.
  • Building strength and community feeling.
  • Relationship with wives and daughters, including care for them.
3. Texts from the Holy Qurʾan and Hadith

Students should know the context, teachings, values, importance and contemporary application of the prescribed texts.

3.1 Prescribed surahs

  • Al-Munafiqun 63:9–11 — charity and accountability to God.
  • Al-Inshirah 94 — consolation in worry and difficulty.
  • At-Tin 95 — belief and righteous deeds for judgement.
  • Al-Asr 103 — faith, deeds, rightful teaching and patience.
  • Al-Maʿun 107 — sincerity rather than superficial religion.
  • An-Nasr 110 — God’s help and support.
  • Al-Falaq 113 — protection from mischief-making and envy.

Study focus: identify the central message, context of revelation, key words, moral principles, and relevance to Muslim life today.

3.2 Prescribed hadith

  • Hadith 5 — rejection of innovation.
  • Hadith 9 — obedience and acting within one’s capacity.
  • Hadith 12 — leaving what does not concern a person.
  • Hadith 20 — modesty / having shame.
  • Hadith 30 — fulfilling religious obligations and respecting limits.
  • Hadith 31 — not being attached to worldly possessions.
  • Hadith 32 — not causing or returning harm.

Study focus: know the teaching, context, ethical application and significance for individuals and communities.

Paper 2 — Development, Sources, Beliefs and Observances

4. The Four Pious Caliphs
  • Conversion of Abu Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthman and ʿAli and their response to the Prophet’s message.
  • Election processes, consultation and circumstances of succession.
  • Character: piety, humility, bravery, strength and kindness.
  • Following the sunnah, administration, social and religious facilities.
  • Responses to difficulties and significance as examples for Muslims today.
  • Individual and combined impact on the early Muslim community.
5. The Qurʾan, Hadith and Muslim beliefs and practices

5.1 The Qurʾan and Hadith

  • Qurʾan as revealed word of God and basis of Islamic thought and action.
  • Revelation, including Laylat al-Qadr.
  • Transmission, collection, preservation and compilation; role of Zayd ibn Thabit.
  • Use of Qurʾan in Muslim life and worship.
  • Hadith as teachings, deeds and sayings of the Prophet (pbuh).
  • Collection, authenticity testing and classifications: sahih, hasan, daʿif and mawduʿ.

5.2 Articles of Faith and Five Pillars

  • Detailed beliefs: tawhid, risalah and akhirah.
  • Shahadah, salah, zakah, sawm in Ramadan and Hajj.
  • For each, explain basis, observance, significance and contribution to Muslim communities.
6. Festivals, rituals and social interactions

6.1 Festivals

  • ʿId al-Fitr: moon sighting, sunnah, prayer, social traditions and charity.
  • ʿId al-Adha: relationship to Hajj, prayer, ummah and renewed commitment.

6.2 Rites of passage

  • Birth and upbringing: aqiqah, family life and learning Islam.
  • Marriage: partner selection, nikah and family life.
  • Death: beliefs, burial preparation, janazah, mourning and remembrance.

6.3 Social interactions

  • Family, care for the elderly and respect for parents.
  • Community roles: mosques, people in need and relationships across differences.
  • Environmental responsibility: care for crops and trees and responses to environmental issues.

Prescribed Text Study Cards

Surah Al-Munafiqun 63:9–11

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تُلْهِكُمْ أَمْوَالُكُمْ وَلَا أَوْلَادُكُمْ عَنْ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ

“O you who believe, let not your wealth and your children divert you from remembrance of Allah…”

Key application: balance family and wealth with remembrance of Allah, give charity before death, and recognise accountability.

Hadith 5 — Rejection of innovation

مَنْ أَحْدَثَ فِي أَمْرِنَا هَذَا مَا لَيْسَ فِيهِ فَهُوَ رَدٌّ

“Whoever introduces into this affair of ours something that does not belong to it, it is rejected.”

Key application: religious practice should be grounded in the authoritative teachings of Islam.

Hadith 12 — Leaving what does not concern us

مِنْ حُسْنِ إِسْلَامِ الْمَرْءِ تَرْكُهُ مَا لَا يَعْنِيهِ

“Part of the perfection of a person’s Islam is leaving alone that which does not concern them.”

Key application: avoid gossip, unnecessary interference and harmful online behaviour.

Hadith 32 — No harm

لَا ضَرَرَ وَلَا ضِرَارَ

“There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm.”

Key application: prevent harm in speech, actions, relationships, public life and digital spaces.
