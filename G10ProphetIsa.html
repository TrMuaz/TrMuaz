Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, students should be able to:

  • Describe the Qur’anic account of Isa’s miraculous birth.
  • Explain the significance of his speech in infancy.
  • Describe the Temple account while identifying its source.
  • Explain the main purposes of his ministry.
  • Identify his Qur’anic miracles and explain “by Allah’s permission.”
  • Distinguish narrative description, religious teaching and source attribution.
A

Qur’anic narrative

Maryam and the virgin birth

A connected account from Maryam’s upbringing to her return with the infant Isa.

Qur’anic account Before Isa’s birth, the wife of ‘Imran dedicated the child in her womb to Allah’s service. When she gave birth to Maryam, Allah accepted Maryam graciously, caused her to grow well, and placed her under the care of Prophet Zakariyya. He found that Allah provided for her in her prayer chamber. Reference: Ali ‘Imran 3:35–37.

Qur’anic account The angels announced that Allah had chosen and purified Maryam. They then gave her good news of al-Masih, Isa son of Maryam. Maryam asked how she could have a child when no man had touched her. The reply directed attention to Allah’s creative command and power: Allah creates what He wills. Reference: Ali ‘Imran 3:42–47.

Qur’anic account Surah Maryam gives a more extended birth narrative. Maryam withdrew from her family to an eastern place. Allah sent His spirit to her in the form of a well-proportioned man; he identified himself as a messenger of her Lord bringing news of a pure boy. She conceived and withdrew to a distant place. Reference: Maryam 19:16–22.

Qur’anic account The pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of a palm tree. In her distress she was comforted: a stream had been placed below her, and shaking the palm trunk would bring down fresh, ripe dates. She was told to eat, drink and be content. When she later returned carrying the child, her people reacted with shock and accusation. Reference: Maryam 19:23–29.

What “virgin birth” means: Isa was conceived and born to Maryam without a human father. In Islam, this is a sign of Allah’s power, not evidence that Isa is divine. Ali ‘Imran 3:59 compares Isa’s creation with Adam’s: both show that Allah creates as He wills.
Limits of the passages: These Qur’anic verses do not state a birth date, the duration of Maryam’s pregnancy, or a named town of birth. Do not turn later details into Qur’anic facts.

Qur’anic account Ali ‘Imran 3:47

“Such is Allah; He creates what He wills.”

Short excerpt from the meaning translated by Saheeh International. The rest of the verse explains that when Allah decrees a matter, He commands it to be.

Ali ‘Imran 3:59 — Summary of the meaning: Isa’s example before Allah is compared with Adam, whom Allah created from dust by His command.

B

Miracle and message

The miracle of speech in infancy

The infant’s words defend Maryam and introduce central teachings about his identity and mission.

Qur’anic account When Maryam returned, her people accused her of an extraordinary wrong and referred to the honour of her family. She did not answer them herself; she pointed to the infant. They objected that a child in the cradle could not normally speak. Isa then spoke by Allah’s power. Reference: Maryam 19:27–30.

Qur’anic account Isa first declared that he was Allah’s servant. He said that Allah had given him Scripture and made him a prophet, made him blessed, instructed him in prayer and charity, and made him dutiful to his mother rather than arrogant or disobedient. He also spoke of peace at birth, death and resurrection. Reference: Maryam 19:30–33.

His speech vindicated Maryam because the child at the centre of the accusation miraculously testified. It also placed his identity within Islamic monotheism: he is Allah’s servant and prophet. Ali ‘Imran 3:46 foretells speech in the cradle, while Al-Ma’idah 5:110 recalls Allah strengthening Isa so that he spoke in infancy and maturity.

C

Source distinction

Experience in the Temple

These accounts are studied with explicit source attribution.

The following Temple episodes are narrated in the Gospels. They are not explicitly described in the Qur’an. They are included as additional historical/religious accounts relevant to the selected topic. They must not be confused with Maryam’s prayer chamber or sanctuary in Qur’an 3:37.

Gospel account According to Luke 2:41–52, when Isa was twelve, his family went to Jerusalem for Passover. After the festival, he remained in Jerusalem while the travelling group left. The narrative includes Joseph as a figure in this Gospel family account; this detail is not being presented as part of the Qur’anic birth narrative.

Gospel account After searching, the family found him in the Temple among the teachers. Luke describes him as listening, asking questions, and giving answers. Those who heard him were amazed at his understanding. The text does not say that he defeated the teachers in a debate or that they could not answer him.

Interpretation or additional account A careful classroom interpretation is that the episode presents unusual understanding and a strong concern with religious matters. This is an interpretation of the Gospel scene, not an event explicitly narrated in the Qur’an.

Another Temple episode: traders and money changers

Gospel account John 2:13–22 gives a separate adult Temple narrative. During Passover, Isa finds sellers of animals and money changers in the Temple area. John says that he drove them out, poured out the coins, overturned tables, and ordered the sellers to remove the goods.

Gospel quotation: John attributes to Jesus the words, “Do not make my Father’s house a house of trade” (John 2:16, ESV). This is Gospel wording and should not be blended into a statement of Islamic creed.

John 2:18–22 continues with a discussion about a sign and “this temple.” The Gospel itself interprets this wording according to its own theology. In Islamic Studies notes, describe it as part of John’s account rather than as Qur’anic teaching.

D

Message and mission

His ministry

What Isa taught, whom he addressed, and how people responded.

Mission to Bani Isra’il

Qur’anic account Isa was sent as a messenger to the Children of Israel. Allah taught him wisdom, the Tawrah and the Injil. He confirmed the Tawrah before him, while Al-Ma’idah 5:46 describes the Injil as containing guidance and light. References: Ali ‘Imran 3:48–49; Al-Ma’idah 5:46.

Worship Allah alone

Qur’anic account Isa called people to obey Allah and worship Him as Lord. Az-Zukhruf 43:63–64 says that he came with wisdom, clarified some disagreements, and directed people to the straight path of worshipping Allah.

Law and clarification

Qur’anic account Isa confirmed the Tawrah and declared that some things previously forbidden would be made lawful for his people. This change was part of his divinely authorised mission, not a personal rejection of revelation. Reference: Ali ‘Imran 3:50.

The disciples

Qur’anic account When Isa sensed rejection, he asked who would support Allah’s cause. The disciples answered that they were Allah’s supporters and affirmed faith. Al-Ma’idah 5:111 also recalls their inspired response of belief and submission. References: Ali ‘Imran 3:52–53; Al-Ma’idah 5:111.

Belief and rejection

Qur’anic account Isa’s clear signs did not produce one response from everyone. As-Saff 61:14 states that a group from Bani Isra’il believed and another group disbelieved. This shows both support and opposition.

Good news of Ahmad

Qur’anic account As-Saff 61:6 records Isa announcing a messenger to come after him named Ahmad. Interpretation or additional account In Islamic understanding, Ahmad refers to Prophet Muhammad (PBUH).

Do not overstate the chronology: The Qur’an does not give a fixed starting age or exact duration for Isa’s ministry. It refers to al-hawariyyun (the disciples), but does not name or number them in these passages.

Qur’anic account Az-Zukhruf 43:63–64 — Summary of the meaning: Isa came with clear proofs and wisdom, clarified some disputed matters, called for obedience, and commanded worship of Allah, his Lord and their Lord.

As-Saff 61:6 — Short excerpt: “I am the messenger of Allah to you.” Saheeh International.

Further references: Ali ‘Imran 3:48–53; Al-Ma’idah 5:46, 111; As-Saff 61:14.

E

Signs from Allah

Miracles and signs

Select each card to reveal its source and exact limits.

“By Allah’s permission” means that these signs were granted and enabled by Allah. Isa did not possess independent divine power. The repeated phrase protects the central Islamic teaching that all power belongs to Allah.
Three different ideas: (1) acts performed through Isa by Allah’s permission; (2) Isa’s miraculous birth without a human father; and (3) the heavenly table sent by Allah in response to Isa’s supplication. The seven cards are categories, not a count of every individual occasion. Gospel miracles such as walking on water or changing water into wine are not part of this Qur’anic list.

Qur’anic account Ali ‘Imran 3:49 — Summary of the meaning: Isa describes the clay bird, healing the blind and leper, raising the dead, and revealing what people ate and stored. The verse repeatedly attributes the extraordinary acts to Allah’s permission.

Al-Ma’idah 5:110 — Summary of the meaning: Allah reminds Isa of divine favour and repeats that the clay bird, healings and raising of the dead occurred by Allah’s permission.

Al-Ma’idah 5:112–115: The disciples ask for the table; Isa warns them to fear Allah, then supplicates. Allah promises to send it and warns against disbelief afterwards.

Glossary

Maryam
The honoured mother of Prophet Isa; called Mary in English.
Isa
A prophet and messenger of Allah sent to Bani Isra’il; called Jesus in English.
Tawrah
The revelation given to Prophet Musa.
Injil
The revelation given by Allah to Prophet Isa.
Bani Isra’il
The Children of Israel, the people to whom Isa was sent.
Al-hawariyyun
Isa’s disciples or devoted supporters; the Qur’an does not name or number them.
Miracle
An extraordinary sign granted by Allah to support a prophet and communicate truth.
Ministry
Isa’s work of teaching, calling, guiding and carrying out his prophetic mission.
Temple
The sacred complex in Jerusalem in the Gospel accounts; not Maryam’s prayer chamber in Qur’an 3:37.
Virgin birth
Isa’s conception and birth to Maryam without a human father.

Common misconceptions

“The virgin birth means Islam teaches that Isa is divine.”

No. Islam treats the birth as a sign of Allah’s creative power. Qur’an 3:59 compares Isa’s creation with Adam’s.

“Every Temple story is found in the Qur’an.”

No. The childhood Temple visit in Luke 2 and the traders episode in John 2 are Gospel narratives. Qur’an 3:37 concerns Maryam’s prayer chamber.

“Isa performed miracles by his own independent power.”

No. Qur’an 3:49 and 5:110 repeatedly state that key signs occurred by Allah’s permission.

“The Qur’an gives the bird species, names healed people and describes the table’s menu.”

No. Those details are not supplied in the cited Qur’anic passages.

“The Qur’an names and numbers Isa’s disciples.”

No. It calls them al-hawariyyun and records their support, but these passages do not give names or a number.

“Luke says Isa defeated the Temple teachers.”

No. Luke says that he listened, asked questions and gave answers, and that people were amazed at his understanding.

Exam skills

Describe

What happened?

Give accurate narrative details in a sensible order. Name the source when needed.

Example: Maryam pointed to the child; Isa spoke and declared himself Allah’s servant.

Explain

Why is it significant?

Connect the event to belief, character, mission or a lesson. Use “because,” “this shows,” or “therefore.”

Example: The speech vindicated Maryam because the infant himself answered the accusation; it also established Isa’s servanthood and prophethood.

Sample revision questions

These are practice prompts, not official Cambridge questions. Open each item to see a suggested answer.

1. Describe the Qur’anic account of Isa’s birth.
Suggested answer: Explain Maryam’s withdrawal, the angelic announcement, her question, conception by Allah’s command, the labour near the palm tree, water and dates, and her return carrying Isa. State that no human father is involved and cite Qur’an 3:42–47 and 19:16–29.
2. Explain two reasons why Isa’s speech in infancy was significant.
Suggested answer: It defended Maryam against accusation, and it immediately identified Isa as Allah’s servant and prophet. It also introduced duties such as prayer, charity and kindness to his mother.
3. How should a student describe Isa’s childhood Temple experience accurately?
Suggested answer: Attribute it to Luke 2:41–52, not to the Qur’an. State that at twelve he remained in Jerusalem and was found listening to teachers, asking questions and giving answers. Avoid claiming that he defeated them.
4. What were the main purposes of Isa’s ministry?
Suggested answer: He called Bani Isra’il to worship and obey Allah, confirmed the Tawrah, received the Injil, clarified disagreements, made lawful some forbidden things by divine authority, and presented clear signs.
5. Explain the phrase “by Allah’s permission” in the miracle accounts.
Suggested answer: The phrase teaches that Allah granted and enabled the signs. Isa was a prophet through whom miracles occurred; he did not possess independent divine power.
